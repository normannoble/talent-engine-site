The problem

Most people documents don't talk to each other.

Each one was written at a different time, by a different person, for a different purpose. So they drift apart, and the business pays for it in every hire and every review.

Job descriptions list duties

So shortlists fill with people who have done the tasks, not people who will deliver what the role is for.

Interviewers test different things

Then they disagree, and the decision comes down to gut feel and whoever argued hardest.

Growth and reviews drift

"What level am I?" and "How do I get promoted?" get answers that are inconsistent, political, or missing.

Fixing that is usually a consulting project of four to eight months. Few companies start one. Talent Engine does it in days.

What the difference looks like

Duties tell you what someone does. Outcomes tell you if it worked.

The same role, written two ways.

A typical job description

Senior Platform Engineer — Responsibilities

  • Manage cloud infrastructure
  • Maintain CI/CD pipelines
  • Participate in the on-call rota
  • Ensure security compliance
  • Mentor junior team members
Talent Engine

Senior Platform Engineer — First 12 months: what must be true

  • Every customer-facing service has an agreed service level, measured on a dashboard the business reads
  • Product teams deploy to production on their own, several times a day, through a paved road this engineer built
  • Technology risk controls pass internal audit and the regulator's review with no high-severity findings
  • Cloud cost per active customer down by at least 20%, with each team seeing its own spend
  • Two engineers can run any part of the platform without this engineer in the room
